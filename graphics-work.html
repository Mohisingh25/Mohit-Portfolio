The toolkit

What each tool actually does here

Not a logo wall. Each of these owns a specific stage of the pipeline, and the handoff between them is what keeps 550+ creatives consistent.

Ps

Photoshop

Key visuals, compositing, retouching, pack mockups

Every sale creative is built here — background art, cut-out packs, shadows and the offer lockup on separate layer groups so any SKU or percentage can be swapped without a rebuild.

Ai

Illustrator

Logo lockups, offer badges, dielines, vector systems

The badge shapes, ribbons, festival motifs and packaging dielines live as vectors, so the same asset prints at 5cm on a jar label and at 1440px on an RCS banner.

Ae

After Effects

Animated banners, GIF ads, offer reveals

Static layouts are handed to AE with layers intact — the discount number counts up, the pack slides in, the CTA pulses. Exported as GIF for email and MP4 for Meta.

Pr

Premiere Pro

Meta ad videos, reels, process films

Farm and factory footage — bilona churning, milking, slow-heating — cut into 15s and 30s ad edits, with stills pulled from the same timeline for static creatives.

Lr

Product photography grade, batch consistency

One develop preset across every pack shot, so ghee gold, honey amber and pickle red stay identical across 9 product lines and three years of shoots.

Fg

Figma

Size systems, banner specs, web handoff

Where the format matrix lives — 1080×1080, 1080×1920, 1440×720, WhatsApp and email — with safe areas marked so nothing important dies in a crop.

The craft

How a sale creative gets built

Six stages, same order every time. Watch the canvas — it runs the whole build on a loop.

  1. Lock the offerBefore a single pixel: the discount, the threshold and the freebie. "Flat 18% OFF" is the headline the whole layout is built around.
  2. Set the gridSame grid every campaign — logo top-centre, offer in the optical middle, CTA in the lower third, product on the baseline. Safe areas marked for every crop.
  3. Drop the packCut-out product on a transparent layer, lit from the same angle as every other SKU, so packs can be swapped between campaigns in minutes.
  4. Dress the festivalThis is the only layer that changes per campaign — matki and peacock feather for Janmashtami, modak and Ganpati for Chaturthi, rakhi thread for Rakhi.
  5. Build the lockupOffer number, percentage, sub-line and CTA as one locked group. Never re-typeset, never re-scaled by eye.
  6. Adapt, don't stretchRe-composed to story, RCS, WhatsApp and email — elements repositioned inside each canvas, never squashed to fit.

Brand thinking

The six rules that keep it consistent

Volume is the hard part. These are the constraints that let a small team ship a full festival campaign without the brand drifting.

R01The offer is the hero, alwaysThe discount sits at a fixed optical size in the same place on every creative. A user scrolling at speed reads the number before they read the festival — that ordering is deliberate and it never changes.
R02Festival changes the world, not the systemBackground art, props and palette shift per occasion. Grid, logo position, badge shape and CTA do not. Twelve campaigns still read as one brand.
R03Cut-outs, not scenesPacks are cut out and lit to a single angle. It means any SKU drops into any campaign without a reshoot — which is what makes a 5-format, 9-SKU campaign possible in a week.
R04One design, five canvasesEverything is built at 1080×1080, then re-composed for story, RCS, WhatsApp and email. Elements move; nothing gets stretched.
R05Bilingual by defaultDevanagari and English share the lockup. Hindi carries the emotion line, English carries the offer — because that is how the audience actually reads.
R06Pack front is a shelf, not a posterPackaging hierarchy is fixed: brand, then product, then variant, then weight, then proof marks. It has to win at arm's length on a Blinkit tile and at 30cm on a kitchen counter.
