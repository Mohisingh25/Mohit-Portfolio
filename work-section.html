The brief & the problem

A heritage food brand with 40+ SKUs across ghee, atta, oils, pickles and breakfast was reading like a generic Shopify catalogue. Trust was the real conversion blocker — buyers wanted proof of purity before price.

UI direction

Cream ground, ghee-brown as the only saturated accent, and field green reserved for proof badges. Product photography sits on warm neutrals so every jar reads as the hero. Badge system (Best Seller, Trending, Must Try) is one shape, one size, colour-coded — so the grid scans in a single sweep instead of shouting.

UX decisions that mattered

  • Trust before priceLab reports, sourcing and batch traceability were pulled out of the footer and placed inside the buying flow — a purity strip sits directly under the add-to-cart, not three clicks away.
  • Category rail as primary navSix pinned category chips (Ghee, Atta, Breakfast, Pickles, Oils, Immunity) replaced a deep mega-menu. Two taps to any SKU on mobile, where 78% of the traffic sits.
  • Bundles as a discovery surface"Buy More. Save More." turned cross-sell into a browse pattern. Kitchen-intent bundles — Cooking Pack, Grandma's Kitchen Essentials — beat discount-first merchandising on AOV.
  • Bilingual voiceDevanagari runs alongside English in the story band. It is not decoration — it is the register the audience actually buys in.

The brief & the problem

A legacy sweets and namkeen maker moving online for the first time. Six product families with wildly different shapes — 60g mini sweets next to 200g modaks next to khakhra packs — and no visual system holding them together.

UI direction

Deep daadi-brown for type, besan yellow as the one call-to-action colour, navy for structural weight. Quicksand keeps it warm and rounded — closer to a mithai box than a SaaS dashboard. Generous cream padding between rows so dense sweets photography never fights itself.

UX decisions that mattered

  • One tile, six familiesA single square category tile carries Mithai, Khakhra, Namkeen, Bhakhri, Mini Sweets and Mobile Khakhra. Same crop, same shadow, same label position — the shelf reads as one brand instead of six suppliers.
  • Weight-first product cardsEvery card leads with grammage (60g, 165g, 200g) because that is the first thing a sweets buyer checks. Price is second, rating third.
  • FAQ as an objection funnelQuestions grouped into The Basics, Our Products, Availability & Distribution, Quality & Manufacturing — mapped to the four reasons people abandon a first order from an unfamiliar food brand.
  • Gram feed as freshness signal"From the gram" sits above the blog. For a heritage brand, live social proves the kitchen is running today, not in 1974.

The brief & the problem

Every agency site in the category looked identical — stock team photo, five service icons, a form. Arohance needed a site that behaved like proof of capability rather than a claim of it.

UI direction

Pure black canvas, Inter at tight tracking, and a violet-cyan glow that only ever appears behind the mark. Cards are 5% white on black with a 1px hairline — depth comes from luminosity, not shadow. Everything animates on a single easing curve so the page feels engineered rather than decorated.

UX decisions that mattered

  • Strip the imagery, keep the lightNo stock photography anywhere. The only visual asset is the logo, rendered as a light source. Nothing to date, nothing to disprove — the design does the arguing.
  • Three-word spineInnovation, Integration, Acceleration. A single scroll section, three beats, no sub-copy. It gives the pitch a rhythm a prospect can repeat back in a meeting.
  • Services as outcomesCards are titled by what the client gets — E-Commerce Optimization, AI-Powered Digital Marketing, Brand Identity Development — not by what the team does.
  • One CTA, repeated"Schedule a meet" appears in the nav and again at the close. No newsletter, no chatbot, no second ask competing with the booking.

The brief & the problem

Innerwear is bought on fit and returned on fit. The old grid sold on photography alone, so shoppers guessed their size, guessed wrong, and the return rate carried the P&L.

UI direction

A single pink (#F06292) does all the work — CTAs, active states, sale flags — against near-white and soft blush. Type is set light and airy with wide line-height. Cards use a 3:4 crop with no hard border, so the grid reads soft rather than clinical.

UX decisions that mattered

  • Fit attributes on the cardSeamless, wirefree, cotton and coverage are surfaced on the grid tile, not buried in a PDP accordion. The filter decision happens before the click.
  • Category before campaignBras, Panties, Camisoles, Shorts sit above the offer banner. Discount traffic converts, but repeat buyers arrive with a category in mind and should not scroll past a sale to reach it.
  • Calm, non-performative imageryProduct-forward crops on a neutral studio ground. The category is bought privately — the interface stays quiet and respectful rather than editorial.
  • Size confidence loopFit guidance is repeated at grid, PDP and cart. Three chances to correct a size before the order ships is three chances to avoid a return.

The brief & the problem

Nine product lines — spot, margin, perpetual futures, convert, lending, bots, copy trading, broker, academy — competing for one homepage. A newcomer and a professional trader needed the same page to work for both.

UI direction

Bibox blue on a light surface grey, with a full dark theme sharing the same token set. DINPro keeps numerals tabular so price columns align to the decimal. Green and red are reserved exclusively for market direction — never for buttons — so the eye never misreads a CTA as a price move.

UX decisions that mattered

  • Two doors, one pageA Newbie Zone entry and a full mega-nav run in parallel. Beginners get a guided path; pros never lose one-click access to Spot API or COIN-M perpetuals.
  • Grouped mega-nav, not a listSeven labelled groups with descriptions instead of a flat dropdown of 30 links. Scanning cost drops from reading to recognising.
  • Market table above the foldGainers, Mainstream, Volume Leaders, Newest, DeFi, GameFi as tabs. Live numbers are the reason people open an exchange homepage — everything else waits.
  • Trust block before feature blockRobust Security, Professional Team, 24/7 Support, Smooth Operation sit ahead of the product tour. In crypto, the security question is answered first or not at all.

The brief & the problem

One site serving two audiences that want opposite things: a parent buying a first robot kit, and a distributor evaluating margins and warranty terms. Splitting into two sites would have split the brand.

UI direction

Yudu violet carries the brand, circuit mint handles every interactive accent, deep green anchors the partner sections. TT Hoves gives geometric, slightly technical letterforms that suit hardware without turning cold. Product renders float on lilac wash with soft ambient shadow so each robot reads as an object, not a photo.

UX decisions that mattered

  • Consumer scroll, partner exitThe main scroll is built for the buyer — product, story, awards. A persistent Partner Program route lets distributors leave the consumer narrative at any point without a second site.
  • Awards as the credibility gateRed Dot, Good Design and India awards sit immediately after the hero. For hardware from an unfamiliar brand, third-party validation has to land before the price does.
  • Catalog by capabilityCodable, Animatronics and Mechanical — not by price band. Parents shop by what the child will learn; the taxonomy matches the intent.
  • Specs as headline copy"38cm, 17 degrees of freedom" is the headline for Zing, not a spec-table row. In hardware, the number is the story.
